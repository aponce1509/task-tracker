# [[The Cerdà Plan]]


> [!note] Definition
> A pioneering 1859 urban expansion project for Barcelona designed by Ildefons Cerdà, credited as one of the founding works of modern "urbanism" focused on hygiene and egalitarianism.

## The Grid System (The Eixample)

1. **Octagonal Blocks**: The city is organized into $113 \times 113$ meter blocks.
2. **Chamfered Corners (Xamfrans)**: 45-degree angled corners at intersections to improve visibility and allow steam-trams to turn easily.
3. **Standardized Widths**: Streets were designed with a standard width of 20 meters to prevent overcrowding.



## Social and Hygienic Goals

1. **Sunlight and Ventilation**: Originally, Cerdà proposed building on only two or three sides of each block to create interior gardens.
2. **Population Density**: Limits on building height were set to ensure every apartment received natural light.
3. **Egalitarianism**: The grid was intended to be uniform, providing the same quality of services and air to the working class as the bourgeoisie.

## Key Implementation Features

1. **The Avenues**: Introduction of major arteries like Gran Via de les Corts Catalanes and Avinguda Diagonal to cut through the grid for faster transit.
2. **Intermodal Hubs**: Strategic placement of markets and hospitals to be accessible from all points of the grid.
3. **Evolution**: Over time, many interior gardens were filled in by developers, deviating from Cerdà's original low-density vision.
