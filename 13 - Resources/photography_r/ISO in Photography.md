# [[ISO in Photography]]

ISO is a numerical rating that represents the light sensitivity of a camera sensor or film. It is one of the three pillars of the **Exposure Triangle**, alongside [[Aperture in Photography|aperture]] and [[Shutter Speed in Photography|shutter speed]].

### Core Functions

- **Sensitivity Control:** ISO determines how much light the sensor needs to create an image. High ISO values allow for photography in low-light environments, while low ISO values are used in bright conditions.
- **Standardization:** The term stands for International Organization for Standardization. It ensures that ISO 100 on one camera produces the same exposure as ISO 100 on another, given identical aperture and shutter speed.
- **Amplification:** In digital cameras, increasing ISO does not change the physical sensitivity of the sensor. Instead, it applies "gain" (electronic amplification) to the signal captured by the sensor to brighten the resulting image.

### The Trade-off: Image Quality vs. Light

- **Low ISO (e.g., 100–200):** Produces the cleanest images with the highest detail, best color accuracy, and maximum dynamic range. This is the "Base ISO" for most cameras.
- **High ISO (e.g., 3200–12800+):** Allows for faster shutter speeds in dark settings but introduces **digital noise**. Noise appears as grainy textures, colored speckles, or a loss of sharpness in shadows.

### Common ISO Ranges and Use Cases

| **ISO Value**   | **Lighting Condition** | **Typical Use Case**                                       |
| --------------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **100 - 200**   | Bright Sunlight        | Landscapes, outdoor portraits, studio lighting.            |
| **400 - 800**   | Overcast / Indoor      | Cloudy days, well-lit interiors, shade.                    |
| **1600 - 3200** | Low Light              | Indoor events, sunsets, handheld evening shots.            |
| **6400+**       | Extreme Low Light      | Night photography, sports in dim arenas, astrophotography. |

### Technical Considerations

- **Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR):** Raising ISO amplifies both the light signal and the inherent electronic background noise. At high ISOs, the noise becomes more prominent relative to the signal.
- **ISO Invariance:** Some modern sensors are "ISO invariant," meaning brightening an underexposed image in post-processing produces nearly the same noise level as increasing the ISO in-camera.
- **Sensor Size Impact:** Larger sensors (Full Frame) generally handle high ISO settings better than smaller sensors (APS-C or Micro Four Thirds) because they have larger pixels that capture more physical light.
