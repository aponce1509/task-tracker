distribute my number of repetitions and series. If there are different aproach

---

Increasing weight and organizing your reps and sets (repetitions and series)
depend on your training goals. Here’s a formal summary with different approaches
and how to know when to increase weight:

---

### 1\. When to Increase Weight

You should increase weight when:

- You can perform the final reps of your last set with little effort or no form
  breakdown.
- You exceed your target rep range consistently (e.g., aiming for 8–12 but doing
  13–14 easily).
- You no longer feel challenged, and there’s no muscle fatigue or pump after
  sets.

**Guideline:** Increase weight by 2.5–10% depending on the exercise. Compound
lifts (e.g., squats, deadlifts) tolerate larger jumps than isolation movements
(e.g., curls).

---

### 2\. Common Training Approaches

#### A. Hypertrophy (Muscle Growth)

- **Sets/Reps**: 3–5 sets of 8–12 reps
- **Rest**: 30–90 seconds
- **Progression**: When you can do 12 reps for all sets, increase weight.
- **Focus**: Time under tension, form, and controlled motion.

#### B. Strength

- **Sets/Reps**: 4–6 sets of 3–6 reps
- **Rest**: 2–5 minutes
- **Progression**: Increase weight once all reps are completed with proper form.
- **Focus**: Heavy loads, longer rest, lower reps.

#### C. Endurance

- **Sets/Reps**: 2–4 sets of 12–20+ reps
- **Rest**: 30–60 seconds
- **Progression**: Add reps or reduce rest, then increase weight when 20+ reps
  feel easy.

#### D. General Fitness (Mixed Goals)

- **Sets/Reps**: 3–4 sets of 8–15 reps
- **Rest**: 1–2 minutes
- **Progression**: Progressive overload via reps first, then weight.

---

### 3\. Methods of Progression

- **Linear Progression**: Add weight every session or weekly.
- **Double Progression**: Increase reps within a range before adding weight
  (e.g., 8–12 reps).
- **Periodization**: Cycle intensity/volume across weeks.
- **Autoregulation**: Adjust based on daily performance and fatigue.

Certainly. Here’s a concise but formal comparison of **hypertrophy**,
**strength**, and **muscular endurance** training across key dimensions:

---

### 1\. Definition & Objective

| **Goal**        | **Definition**                                                                 |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Hypertrophy** | Increase in muscle size through enlargement of muscle fibers (mainly Type II). |
| **Strength**    | Maximize the amount of force a muscle or muscle group can exert.               |
| **Endurance**   | Improve a muscle’s ability to perform repeated contractions over time.         |

---

### 2\. Repetitions, Sets, and Intensity

| **Variable**          | **Hypertrophy** | **Strength** | **Endurance** |
| --------------------- | --------------- | ------------ | ------------- |
| **Repetitions**       | 6–12            | 1–6          | 12–20+        |
| **Sets**              | 3–5             | 4–6          | 2–4           |
| **Load (%1RM)**       | ~65–80%         | 80–95%       | ~40–60%       |
| **Rest Between Sets** | 30–90 seconds   | 2–5 minutes  | 30–60 seconds |

---

### 3\. Muscle Fiber Type Targeted

| **Type**        | **Description**                             |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| **Hypertrophy** | Primarily Type II (fast-twitch)             |
| **Strength**    | Primarily Type II (with neural adaptations) |
| **Endurance**   | Mainly Type I (slow-twitch)                 |

---

### 4\. Adaptations

| **Aspect**             | **Hypertrophy**      | **Strength**                           | **Endurance**                      |
| ---------------------- | -------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| **Primary Adaptation** | Muscle size increase | Neural efficiency + muscle recruitment | Mitochondrial density, capillarity |
| **Rate of Fatigue**    | Moderate             | High                                   | Low                                |
| **Recovery Demand**    | Moderate to high     | High                                   | Low to moderate                    |

---

### 5\. Suitable Population/Goal

| **Goal**                                                | **Approach** |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------ |
| Bodybuilding                                            | Hypertrophy  |
| Powerlifting, Olympic Lifting                           | Strength     |
| Sports with repetitive actions (e.g., running, cycling) | Endurance    |

---

### Summary

- **Hypertrophy** = focus on muscle growth (volume > intensity).
- **Strength** = focus on maximal force output (intensity > volume).
- **Endurance** = focus on sustaining output over time (volume with low
  intensity).

For **climbing**, you should train **both strength and endurance**, but the
emphasis depends on the type of climbing you do:

---

### 1\. Bouldering (Short, Powerful Routes)

- **Focus**: **Maximal Strength & Power**
- **Why**: Routes are short and intense, demanding high force output in a few
  moves.
- **Training**:
  - Weighted pull-ups
  - Campus board (explosive power)
  - Max hangs (fingerboard)
  - Low-rep strength training (3–6 reps)

---

### 2\. Sport Climbing (Longer, Sustained Routes)

- **Focus**: **Muscular Endurance + Strength**
- **Why**: Requires sustained contractions over minutes and the ability to
  recover on the wall.
- **Training**:
  - Repeaters on a fingerboard (e.g., 7s hang / 3s rest x 6–7 reps)
  - ARC training (long, low-intensity climbs for forearm endurance)
  - Moderate rep sets (8–15) for general muscular endurance

---

### 3\. Traditional (Trad) or Alpine Climbing

- **Focus**: **General Endurance + Technique + Mental Stamina**
- Less intensity per move, but high cumulative fatigue.
- Emphasize full-body muscular and aerobic endurance.

---

### Recommendation

- Train **strength** for finger, core, and pulling power.
- Train **endurance** to handle long routes and reduce pump.
- Use **periodization**: cycle between strength and endurance blocks depending
  on your climbing season.
