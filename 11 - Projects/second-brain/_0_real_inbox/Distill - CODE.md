# [[Distill - CODE]]

## Definition

Distilling is the process of extracting the "essence" of a note. It is the transition point where you move from **Divergence** (collecting) to **Convergence** (focusing) ([[Convergence and Divergence]]), ensuring your future self can grasp the core idea in seconds.

It's also important that splitting the cleaning from the capturing we save time in non useful captures.

## Core Strategy

1. **The Principle of Delayed Polishing**: Do not polish a note immediately upon capture. Wait until you have a specific need or project to avoid wasting time on low-value information.
2. **The "Signal vs. Noise" Filter**: Focus on making the most important parts of the note stand out while removing the filler: [[Progressive Summarisation]]

## Distilling Internal Notes (Your Thoughts)

1. **Add New Perspectives**: Re-read your initial reflections and add updated angles or connections to other ideas.
2. **Add to an Index**: Link the note to a higher-level Map of Content (MOC) or index to ensure it is findable.
3. **Refine the Language**: Clear up ambiguous phrasing from the initial capture.

## Distilling External Notes (Others' Content)

1. **Aggressive Trimming**: Remove any parts of the source material that aren't relevant to your goals.
2. **Grouping and Synthesis**: Use AI or manual effort to group related highlights and create a concise summary.
3. **Active Learning**: Rephrase the content in your own words (the Feynman Technique) to ensure true understanding rather than just "collecting" words.
4. **Extraction**: Identify the foundational principles or "bases" of the text and highlight them for quick review.
5. **Linking and Connecting**: Create Index, MoC or Syllabus to create knowledge and to establish the learned concepts.
