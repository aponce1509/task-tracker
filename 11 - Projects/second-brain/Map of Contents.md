# [[Map of Contents]]

> [!note] Definition
> A Map of Contents (MOC) is a fluid, non-hierarchical note that acts as a curated hub or "table of contents" to link related notes together, facilitating navigation within a digital personal knowledge management system.

## The Role of an MOC

1. Acts as a "launchpad" for specific topics or projects.
2. Provides a bird's-eye view of a knowledge area without forcing a rigid folder structure.
3. Allows for "emergent" organization, where the map grows as the number of notes increases.

## Creation Process

1. Identify a "cluster" of related notes that have reached a critical mass.
2. Create a new note titled after the broad topic.
3. Link the individual notes within the MOC.
4. Add brief context or narrative around the links to explain their relationships.

## MOC vs. Index

1. An Index is typically a flat, alphabetical list of terms.
2. An MOC is curated and organized by relevance or logic.
3. Indexes are static, while MOCs are dynamic "workbenches" for thinking.
4. MOCs allow for "nesting" where one MOC links to another higher-level MOC.

## Benefits

1. Reduces "link rot" and the feeling of being overwhelmed by a "flat" list of notes.
2. Encourages "bottom-up" thinking rather than "top-down" categorization.
3. Facilitates faster retrieval of information through structured paths.
