# [[Capture Criteria How to Avoid Keeping Too Much (or Too Little)]]


> Once you have identified the kinds of questions you want your Second Brain to
> answer, it’s time to choose specifically which pieces of information will be
> most useful.
>
> Tiago Forte. Building a Second Brain (p. 72).

Describe a la perfección los problemas con los que me encuentro a la hora de
lidiar con referencias.

## Takeaways

<!-- NOTE: key capturing takeaways -->

- Don't save the whole content

  > If you try to save every piece of material you come across, you run the risk
  > of inundating your future self with tons of irrelevant information. At that
  > point, ==your Second Brain will be no better than scrolling through social
  > media.==

- Keep only things that:
  - Inspiring
  - Useful
  - Personal
  - Surprising
- Keep what resonate
- We want to solve:
  - dive straight into the first piece of content they see, read it voraciously
  - open dozens of tabs in their web browser and feel a pang of guilt at all those interesting resources they haven’t been able to get to

## The context given

<!-- NOTE: evidences/question How do we manage a interesting reference? -->

Imagine you come across a blog post during your web browsing that details how a
marketing expert you respect runs her campaigns. You’re hooked: this is the kind
of material you’ve been looking for! Finally, the master reveals her secrets!

<!-- NOTE: evidences/evidence By default we save it all -->

Your first instinct might be to save the article in its entirety. It’s
high-quality information, so why not preserve all of it? The problem is, it’s an
in-depth how-to article that is thousands of words long.

<!-- NOTE: evidences/evidence Problem of this perspective -->

Even if you spend the twenty or thirty minutes it would take to consume it now,
in the future you’ll just have to spend all that time reading it again, since
you’ll have forgotten most of the details.

<!-- NOTE: evidences/evidence Problem of just bookmarking it -->

You also don’t want to just bookmark the link and save it to read later, because
then you won’t know what it contains in the first place!

<!-- NOTE: evidences/conclusion We need to be prepared to deal when we enter in learning mode -->

This is where most people get stuck. They either dive straight into the first
piece of content they see, read it voraciously, but quickly forget all the
details, or they open dozens of tabs in their web browser and feel a pang of
guilt at all those interesting resources they haven’t been able to get to.
