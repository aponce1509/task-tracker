# [[Intermediate and Final Products]]

> [!abstract] The Lego bricks of my work
> - An intermediate product is an output of a project that is not the final result but has future value.
> - Small units keep their value even when the project is cancelled.
> - Catalog: conceptual, technical, logistic (intermediate) and final deliverables.

> [!info] Where they are recorded
> - During a project: the `## Intermediate products` section of its journal (`journal.sh product "<what>"`), see [[Task Management]].
> - At the close: `journal.sh close` lists them; the ones worth keeping get their own note.

## Definition

1. Artifacts and outputs of the project lifecycle that are not the final result.
2. Content with potential future value for other projects or iterations.
3. It can be a mention of an internal product that does not live in Obsidian.

Producing them is the exit of the loop: [[Express - CODE]]. They are small on purpose: [[Dial Down the Scope]].

## Utility

1. Prevents reinventing the wheel: the logic of previous tasks stays.
2. Gives a modular library of building blocks for future work.
3. Bridges the gap between raw external notes and polished final deliverables.

## The catalog of outputs

1. Conceptual & strategic (intermediate)
	1. **Distilled notes**: curated insights from external sources or internal brainstorming.
	2. **Outtakes**: strategic leftovers, analysis or data that did not fit the scope but keep value.
	3. **Decision logs (ADRs)**: why a technical decision was made.
	4. **Questions & doubts**: structured inquiries to probe the system architecture.
	5. **Drafts**: preliminary versions of documents, articles or reports.
	6. **Brainstorming maps**: structured logic or diagrams that solve one problem.
2. Technical & code assets (intermediate/final)
	1. **Scripts & algorithms**: small automation tools or optimised logic blocks.
	2. **Internal libraries**: reusable code modules extracted from a feature.
	3. **Design patterns**: templates for recurring architectural problems.
	4. **Prompts & agents**: system instructions and AI configurations for specific tasks.
	5. **Prototypes**: early models, wireframes or code snippets.
3. Project logistics & definition (intermediate)
	1. **Requirements (PRDs)**: product definitions, feature sets or job descriptions.
	2. **Technical debt log**: what needs fixing to avoid future interest payments.
	3. **Workflows & models**: diagrams of how data and users move through a system.
	4. **Agenda & meeting outcomes**: results of syncs that drive the next action.
4. Final deliverables (final products)
	1. **Final documents**: PDFs, Word docs or slides for stakeholders.
	2. **Core components**: the main feature or system shipped.
	3. **Implementation details**: the how-to for deployment and maintenance.
	4. **Curriculums & reviews**: overviews of a subject or of the project performance.
	5. **Post-mortems / RCAs**: analysis of failures to prevent recurrence.
	6. **Proofs of concept (PoC)**: a minimal implementation that proves a technical hypothesis.
	7. **RFCs**: a document to gather feedback before implementation.
	8. **Onboarding guides**: a packet to get a new developer up to speed on the codebase.
