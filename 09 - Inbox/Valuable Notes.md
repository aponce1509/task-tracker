# [[Valuable Notes]]

This is a foundational topic in Personal Knowledge Management (PKM). Many people collect information, but few build a system that _compounds_ over time. To do that, you need to understand the lifecycle of a note.

Here is a dissection of how **General Captures**, **Distilled Notes**, **Permanent Notes**, and **Evergreen Notes** function and link together to create a "second brain."

---

### 1. The Funnel: From Noise to Signal

Think of these four concepts not as separate buckets, but as stages in a refining process. You are moving from high-volume, low-value noise (Capture) to low-volume, high-value insight (Evergreen).

#### **Stage 1: General Captures (Fleeting Notes)**

This is the intake valve. It is the raw, unprocessed material of your life.

- **Definition:** Anything that catches your attention—a stray thought, a quote from a podcast, a task, a URL, or a screenshot.
- **Characteristics:** Messy, context-free, and temporary.
- **The Goal:** Capture quickly so you don't lose the idea, but do not process it yet.
- **Lifespan:** Very short. These should be deleted or processed within 24-48 hours. If they stay here, they become digital clutter.

#### **Stage 2: Distilled Notes (Literature/Reference Notes)**

This is where you engage with a specific source.

- **Definition:** Notes taken while consuming content (books, articles, videos). You are distilling the author's ideas into your own words.
- **Characteristics:** Summarized, source-centric, and structured.
- **The Goal:** To understand _what the author said_. You are strictly comprehending, not yet creating new theories.
- **The Link:** You often pull "General Captures" into this stage when you sit down to actually read that article you saved.

---

### 2. The Core: The Atomic Unit of Knowledge

Once you have distilled information, you need to turn it into _knowledge_. This is where the confusion between Permanent and Evergreen notes often lies. They are siblings, but with different philosophies.

#### **Stage 3: Permanent Notes (The Archive)**

- **Definition:** A single, atomic idea written in a way that is self-explanatory.
- **Characteristics:**
    - **Atomic:** One idea per note.
    - **Autonomous:** The note makes sense even if you lose the original source.
    - **Immutable-ish:** Once written, they tend to stay as a record of your thought at that time.
- **The Goal:** To create a library of thoughts you have had, separated from the books they came from.
#### **Stage 4: Evergreen Notes (The Living Organism)**

This concept, popularized by Andy Matuschak, is an evolution of the Permanent Note.
- **Definition:** Notes that are written and organized to evolve, contribute, and accumulate over time, across projects.
- **Characteristics:**
    - **Densely Linked:** Their value comes from their connections to other notes, not just their content.
    - **Living:** You constantly revisit, edit, and refactor them. An Evergreen note is never "done."
    - **Concept-Oriented:** Titled like a claim or an API (e.g., _"Spaced repetition creates structural changes in the brain"_ rather than _"Memory Techniques"_).
- **The Link:** An Evergreen note often _starts_ as a Permanent note, but as you link it to other ideas, it grows "moss" (connections) and becomes deeper.

---

### 3. Comparison: Permanent vs. Evergreen

While often used interchangeably, the nuance is in the _intent_.

| **Feature**      | **Permanent Note (Classic Zettelkasten)** | **Evergreen Note (Matuschak/Modern PKM)** |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| **Primary Goal** | archival of distinct thoughts             | Development of thinking over time         |
| **Editing**      | Rare; tends to be static                  | Constant; refactoring is encouraged       |
| **Structure**    | Linear/Index-based                        | Associative/Graph-based                   |
| **Analogy**      | A filing cabinet of distinct index cards  | A garden where plants grow and tangle     |

---

### 4. Linking It Together: The Workflow

How do you actually do this? Here is the flow of data through your system:

1. **Capture:** You are walking the dog and have an idea: _"Maybe burnout is just a lack of autonomy."_ You capture this into your inbox (General Capture).
2. **Distill:** Later, you read a book on psychology to investigate. You take notes on the book, summarizing the chapters (Distilled Notes).
3. **Synthesize (Permanent):** You look at your book notes and your dog-walking idea. You write a new, standalone note: _"Autonomy is a stronger predictor of job satisfaction than workload."_ This is now a Permanent Note.
4. **Evolve (Evergreen):** Six months later, you read about "learned helplessness." You go back to your Autonomy note and link them. You rewrite the Autonomy note to include this nuance. It has now become an **Evergreen Note**—it is living, breathing, and connecting disparate ideas.

### **The "Why"**

Most people stop at **General Captures** (hoarding links) or **Distilled Notes** (summarizing books).

- If you stop at Captures, you are a **librarian** of unread things.
- If you stop at Distilled, you are a **scribe** for other people's ideas.
- When you move to **Permanent/Evergreen**, you become a **thinker**. You are no longer storing information; you are building a lattice of your own understanding.
